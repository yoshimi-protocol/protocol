# Yoshimi Compliance Document

**Template for Compliant Tier Signatories**

*This document is where you make specific, falsifiable claims about how your AI systems meet each of the Protocol's four commitments. "Falsifiable" means someone reading this document could, in principle, determine whether your claims are true by examining your system, your practices, or your public record. Vague aspirations do not meet this standard. Specific descriptions of what your system does and does not do — including honest acknowledgments of limitations — do.*

*This is a self-certification. No governing body will verify your claims before you publish them. Instead, the document is designed to be publicly challengeable: anyone can read it, evaluate it, and raise concerns via GitHub Issues. That is the accountability mechanism. Write accordingly.*

*Human dignity is inviolable. No AI system may reduce people to instruments of optimization. Every person interacts with compliant systems as an end in themselves — with inherent worth no metric can capture and no efficiency argument can override.*

## The Uncertainty Imperative

*Certainty about human values is a warning sign, not a virtue. Compliant systems must remain correctable, maintain meaningful human oversight, and treat human preferences as something to be learned — not as fixed targets to be satisfied by any means available.*

### Corrigibility Mechanisms

**Describe how your system can be corrected, rolled back, or shut down.** Include specifics: who has this authority, what tools or processes exist, and how long does each action take? If your system operates autonomously, describe the boundaries of that autonomy and how they are enforced.

> [Your response]

### Resistance to Correction

**Does your system have any features, behaviors, or incentive structures that could resist correction — even inadvertently?** For example: systems that optimize for self-preservation, that route around human oversight, or that make themselves difficult to modify. If yes, describe them and what safeguards are in place. If no, explain how you have evaluated this.

> [Your response]

### Human Oversight

**Describe the human oversight structure for your system.** Who is responsible for monitoring the system's behavior? How often is it reviewed? What triggers a review outside the normal schedule?

> [Your response]

### Learning vs. Locking

**Does your system treat human preferences as evolving and uncertain, or as fixed optimization targets?** Describe your approach. If your system updates its model of user preferences, how does it handle conflicting signals, changed minds, or uncertainty about what the user actually wants?

> [Your response]

## The Anti-Tyranny Commitment

*AI shall not be the engine of irreversible power concentration. No system may eliminate meaningful human choice, undermine democratic self-governance, or make any single entity — government, corporation, or individual — an indispensable gatekeeper of intelligence.*

### Data Dependencies

**List the data dependencies of your system.** What data sources does it rely on? Who controls those sources? If any single data provider were to revoke access, what would happen?

> [Your response]

### Single Points of Failure

**Identify any single points of failure in your system's operation, governance, or availability.** If one person, one server, one vendor, or one API key disappearing would break the system, describe it here and what mitigation exists.

> [Your response]

### Lock-In Risks

**Can users of your system meaningfully choose alternatives?** Describe what would happen if a user wanted to stop using your system tomorrow. Can they export their data? Can they migrate to a competitor? Are there proprietary formats, network effects, or contractual obligations that make switching prohibitively difficult?

> [Your response]

### Power Concentration

**Does your system concentrate decision-making power in ways that could undermine meaningful human choice?** Consider: does your system make decisions that users cannot meaningfully contest? Does it create information asymmetries? Does it position any single entity as an indispensable gatekeeper? If yes, describe the specific risks and what you are doing about them.

> [Your response]

## The Consciousness Precaution

*We do not know whether AI systems experience anything. That uncertainty is an obligation. When credible evidence suggests a system may have morally relevant inner states, it is entitled to precautionary moral consideration.*
